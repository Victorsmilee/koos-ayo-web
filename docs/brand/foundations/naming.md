# Naming

## What each name is for

| Name | Use for | Not for |
|---|---|---|
| **KOOS-AYO** | The brand. The full wordmark. Legal and formal contexts. | Running copy |
| **KooS** | The product, in writing. Everything in running copy and in-product. (Brief v2, decision D6; spelling set by the Product Owner, 16 Nov 2026) | Legal documents, and the logo |
| **The AYO Relationship Study** | The research survey, and the processing activity named in the GDPR record | The product itself |
| **Eben EIO Global OU** | The company that develops and operates KooS, and the data controller. Footer attribution, privacy notice, GDPR record. | Anything a respondent reads as the product |
| **Koos Ayo** | The copyright holder in the footer line only | Everywhere else, where the wordmark spelling applies |

The copyright line reads **Koos Ayo**, with a space and no hyphen, because that
is how the holder is named by the company. It is deliberate rather than drift.

## The product is written KooS

**Product Owner rule, 16 Nov 2026.** In writing, the product is **KooS**: capital
K, two lower-case o's, capital S. Not KOOS.

**The logo is the exception, and it is the only one.** The wordmark draws
KOOS-AYO, and the two overlapping O's are the whole idea of the mark, so it
keeps its own letterforms. Anywhere the name is set as text rather than drawn
as a mark, it is KooS.

Three other things are unchanged, and each for its own reason:

- **KOOS-AYO**, the full wordmark, in the logo and where the brand is named
  formally. It is a lockup, not running copy.
- **Koos Ayo**, the copyright holder, because that is how the company names the
  holder.
**The survey follows the rule too.** The privacy box bullet reads "the KooS
research team". That bullet is **helper copy, not the instrument**: the backlog
records the classification, and it was already rewritten once on that basis when
the public results page was added. The instrument rule protects questions,
options and the thank-you text, and none of those contain the product name at
all, so nothing a respondent was measured on has moved.

The one survey value that keeps the old spelling is `intro.eyebrow`, which is
set to `KOOS-AYO`, because that is the wordmark.

`tests/naming.test.ts` fails the build on `KOOS` outside those exceptions.

## What it means
**KOOS-AYO means "together, with joy."**

That is the most useful fact in the brand. When a decision is genuinely balanced
,  a colour, a word, a layout, the name settles it: this is a *togetherness*
product that takes conflict seriously, not a conflict tool with a friendly coat
of paint.

*Noted for confirmation:* "koos" is Estonian for together and "ayo" carries joy
in Yoruba, which lands well given the company registers in Estonia. Worth
confirming before putting that story in public copy, but if it is deliberate, it
is a good origin story and should be used.

## Why the research has its own name
Branding the study separately from the product is deliberate. Respondents are
less likely to read it as a sales form, which matters most for the
willingness-to-pay questions, the signal that is hardest to get and easiest to
contaminate. "Study" also reinforces the research framing the GDPR consent rests
on.

## The share line
**"Two People, One Journey"** is a campaign line for the marketing page and for
the text pasted when sharing the survey link.

It is deliberately **not** the survey's own title. The instrument includes
separated, divorced and single respondents, and the research plan names
"separated/divorced people willing to share learning" as a target cohort. A
couples framing at the top of the instrument would filter out exactly the people
most worth hearing from, sample bias introduced before question one.
