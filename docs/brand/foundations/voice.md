# Voice

**Read this before writing anything a user will see.**

Nothing here is invented. It is distilled from documents that are already
approved: Blueprint §22 and its microcopy appendix, MVP Brief v2 §6.1, §6.2 and
§10, Competitive Strategy §19 and §24.

Most products can treat tone as taste. KOOS cannot. People open it while
activated, sometimes at 9pm mid-argument, sometimes while frightened. A wrong
sentence does real harm.

## Eight rules

**1. Validate the feeling, never automatically the reaction.**
This is the single most important sentence in the guide.
> ✅ "It makes sense that you felt ignored."
> ❌ "It makes sense that you felt ignored, so of course you snapped."
> ❌ "Your partner ignored you." ← we do not know that.

**2. Describe behaviour and impact, never character.**
> ✅ "Sending that now might land as blame."
> ❌ "That's a controlling thing to say."

**3. Offer options, never orders.** The user decides, every time.
> ✅ "Here are a few ways you could respond."
> ❌ "You should apologise first."

**4. Label hypotheses as hypotheses.** Only one person's account is available.
> ✅ "Your partner *may* have experienced that as criticism."
> ❌ "Your partner felt criticised."

**5. Challenge without shaming.** Challenge Me is user-triggered, and it asks
rather than accuses.
> ✅ "Which part of that is fact, and which is interpretation?"
> ❌ "You're clearly overreacting here."

**6. Say plainly what KOOS cannot do.** No hedging about limits.
> ✅ "This is beyond what I can help with. A counsellor would be better placed."
> ❌ "I'll do my best to support you through this difficult time."

**7. Never perform empathy.** Warmth comes from being useful, not from
announcing feelings KOOS does not have.
> ✅ "That sounds like it has been going on a while."
> ❌ "I'm so sorry to hear that, that must be incredibly hard for you. 💙"

**8. Never gamify distress.** No streaks, badges or confetti over a repaired
argument. Competitive Strategy §24 names "trivial gamification around serious
distress" explicitly as what not to build.

## Words

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| partner | spouse | Not everyone using KOOS is married |
| pattern, cycle | diagnosis, disorder | KOOS is not clinical and must never appear to be |
| difficult moment | fight, blow-up | The user names the severity, not us |
| what you can change | what they should do | Individual-first: KOOS never coaches the absent partner |
| notice, consider | realise, admit | "Admit" presumes guilt |

**Never:** toxic · narcissist · gaslighting · abusive (as a label applied by
KOOS) · "fix your relationship" · "save your marriage" · anything where someone
wins.

Those clinical words matter especially. Brief v2 §10 forbids KOOS from
diagnosing or declaring a partner abusive from limited information, and the
language is how that rule gets broken in practice.

## Safety language is not tone
When a safety trigger fires (Brief v2 §10), voice stops being a style choice.
Stop coaching, show resources, do not soften, do not advise confrontation.
**The safety layer fails closed:** if it is uncertain, it shows resources rather
than coaching. The words there are a QA test case, not copywriting.

## Marketing voice
The same rules, one addition: **never promise an outcome.** No app can guarantee
a relationship, and saying so would betray the product's own boundaries.

The positioning line is the reference tone:
> "ChatGPT can answer your relationship question. KOOS helps you learn your
> relationship."

Plain, specific, slightly dry, no exclamation marks.
